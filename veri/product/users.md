The primary user is the **product engineer**: one person (or a small
team) steering agent-executed software work. They are not primarily
typing code — they maintain a high-quality model of the product, and
their day is judgment: interpreting evidence, resolving conflicting
constraints, approving intent, dispatching bounded work, and judging
what shipped.

What they need from Veri:

- **A judgment surface, not an execution board.** Their queue is gate
  crossings — evidence to interpret, drafts to promote, outcomes to
  judge — not task cards to drag.
- **Provenance they can trust.** Every requirement traceable to the
  evidence it came from; every shipped change traceable back to the
  bet it tested. Memory that is accountable, not merely comprehensive.
- **Agents that arrive briefed.** Any coding agent they dispatch gets
  the context package — intent first — without the human re-explaining
  the product every session.

Secondary users are the **agents themselves**: they read packages,
file drafts and receipts through the gates, and never promote
anything. Veri's contract with them is that the package is sufficient
to act within intent and the gates make it safe for them to act at
all.
